Australia's National Parks

Are we protecting the right places, and who uses them?

Australia protects more than a fifth of its land. This page looks at how much of that is national park, where it is, and who lives near it. National parks are one kind of protected land, so we start with the whole picture.

The road to 30%

Four places are already past 30%: the ACT, Tasmania, Western Australia and South Australia. Queensland and New South Wales are about 20 points short. Across the whole country, about 25% was protected by July 2026, and roughly 34.3 million more hectares are needed to reach 30%.

Who actually goes?

Counting visitors is harder than mapping parks. We found park-by-park counts from only three sources: the NSW National Parks and Wildlife Service, Tasmania's Parks and Wildlife Service and Parks Australia. Some states publish only state-wide totals, and for others we could not find any. Each agency counts in its own way, so their numbers should not be ranked against each other.

New South Wales counted 65.6 million visits to its parks in 2024/25, the highest of any survey since 2008. Blue Mountains alone had 7.8 million, about 12% of the total. Several of the busiest parks are inside Sydney, such as Lane Cove, Sydney Harbour and Garigal.

Visits fell in 2020, the COVID-19 year, then climbed past the old peak. Children are a smaller share of visitors than before: 25% of visits in 2018 and 19% in 2024/25.

Tasmania's Parks and Wildlife Service counts visitors at 14 reference sites. All 12 sites shown here had more visits in 2025-26 than in 2020-21. The total for 13 of the sites rose from 864,000 to 1.2 million. Maria Island grew most, from 33,000 to 88,000 visits, nearly three times as many.

Because agencies count differently, the last chart compares each place only with its own busiest year. Half of the Tasmanian sites (six of 12) matched or beat their busiest year in 2025-26. Uluru is the exception at Parks Australia: in 2024-25 it had 68% of its 2018-19 visits. The 2022-23 annual report said Uluru was the park most affected by the slow return of international visitors.

Most national parks are small, and a few are giants

Australia has nearly 800 national parks, and 71% of them are in Queensland and New South Wales. The typical park covers about 65 square kilometres. But 87 parks are bigger than 1,000 square kilometres, and the ten biggest hold about a third of all national park area. The giants are in the dry centre and the north. The Munga-Thirri (Simpson Desert) park in South Australia covers 36,000 square kilometres, more than half the size of Tasmania.

Queensland has the most national parks (317), but the typical one is only about 35 square kilometres. The Northern Territory has just 18, and the typical one is about 1,250 square kilometres. The number of parks partly reflects how each state names its reserves, so it does not measure how much land a state protects.

What it adds up to

National parks cover only 5.5% of Australia, a small part of the 22.6% that is protected. Protection is uneven between states and between regions, and most protected land is far from most people. Visits are mostly up where they are counted, but only three sources publish park-by-park numbers. As Australia aims for 30%, the question is not only how much land is protected, but which land.

National parks are only part of the protected land

About 22.6% of Australia's land is protected (June 2024). Only about a quarter of that is national park, which is 5.5% of the whole country. Indigenous Protected Areas, where Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people manage their own land for conservation, cover twice as much (11.2%).

Not every state protects the same share

Tasmania protects 43% of its land and the ACT 56%. New South Wales and Queensland protect less than 11% each. Western Australia and South Australia are the only large states already past 30%, the national goal for 2030. That goal applies to the whole country, so here it is only a yardstick.

Most protected land is far from most people

New South Wales and Victoria are home to 57% of Australians but hold only 7% of the country's protected land. Western Australia, the Northern Territory and South Australia hold 82% of it and are home to 19% of the people.

Another way to see the same gap is to count how many people live for every square kilometre of protected land. In the ACT it is about 370. In Victoria it is about 180, and in New South Wales about 100. In the Northern Territory it is fewer than one.

Is it the right land?

Australia is divided into 89 natural regions, each with its own landscape and wildlife. Eighty-seven are on land. Twenty-seven of them have less than 10% protected, and together they cover about 40% of the country. At the other end, the west of Tasmania is 86% protected.

What matters is not only how much is protected, but what kind. Across the country, 58% of protected land is in category VI, where sustainable use of natural resources can continue, and 23% is in category II, which is national park. In 9 of the 12 least protected regions, national-park land is the biggest part of what is protected, but it is only 1% to 3% of the region.
